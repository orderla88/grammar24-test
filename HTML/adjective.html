Introduction

Adjective is a word, that describes a property of real or abstract object. There are many adjectives, so for convenience we divide them into categories.

Adjective Categories
Category Example
Age new, old, modern
Color green, white, yellow

Opinion handsome, hard, terrible
Origin Chinese, urban, foreign
Quality wooden, metal, plastic
Shape square, oval, flat
Size small, large, tall

Comparison Degrees

To compare properties of several objects, we use three degrees of comparison.

  1. Positive degree – when compared objects have same amount of particular property, put the property (adjective) between structure as…as;

    She is as tall as her mother.
    This tree is as old as me.

  2. Comparative degree – when one of compared objects has more/less of some property than the other(s):
    • add suffix -er to adjectives with 1-2 syllables;*

      He is taller than his brother.
      My car is faster than yours.

    • put word more/less before adjectives with 3 or more syllables;

      Is math more difficult than science?
      This issue is less important right now.

  3. Superlative degree – when one of compared objects has the most/least of some property;
    • add suffix -est to adjectives with 1-2 syllables;*

      She is the tallest in her family.
      It is the smallest dog I've seen.

    • put word most/least before adjectives with 3 or more syllables;

      The most beautiful girl I've seen.
      Which cafe is the least expensive?

Summary
Positive Comparative Superlative
tall taller tallest
rare rarer rarest
difficult more difficult most difficult
urgent less urgent least urgent
* This is true for two-syllable adjectives ending in -y, -ow, -le, -er, -ure. Others often use more/most. If you are unsure which way it is, use the latter.

Below are some exceptions to the rules above:

  • Regardless of syllable number, all adjectives ending in -ed, -ing, -ful, -less form comparative and superlative degrees with more/most accordingly.

    Positive Comparative Superlative
    tilted more tilted most tilted
    boring more boring most boring
    graceful more graceful most graceful
    shameless more shameless most shameless

  • Following six adjectives have irregular comparative and superlative forms. We need to memorize them.

    Positive Comp-ve Sup-ve
    bad worse worst
    good/well better best
    far farther farthest
    little less least
    much/many more most

Adjective Types

There are following types of adjectives:

  • Attributive – come before noun;

    The gray and gloomy clouds hid the sun.
    The old and shabby cabin was empty.

  • Appositive – come after noun;

    Clouds, gray and gloomy, hid the sun.
    The cabin, old and shabby, was empty.

  • Participial – participle forms of verb describing a noun;

    exciting news,   relaxing music  
    excited crowd,   relaxed state

  • Proper – formed from proper nouns, capitalized;

    Japanese culture,   Confucian ideas,
    French fashion,   Victorian era;

  • Nominal 1 – function as noun, preceded by article 'the';

    The rich will never hear the poor.
    The Chinese celebrate Lunar New Year.

  • Compound – consist of 2-3 words joined by a hyphen;

    narrow-minded,   eco-friendly,
    English-speaking,   two-year-old;

1 The are also called nominalized adjectives.

From above list, compound adjectives need more elaboration.

  • hyphen is inserted to denote the modifier, thus preventing confusion;

    man-eating shark vs man eating shark
    good-looking girl vs good looking girl;

  • the common forms are 'adjective/adverb + participle' and 'noun + adjective/participle'.

    short-haired, fast-growing, well-known;
    snow-white, time-saving, home-made;

  • form with noun always uses singular form of it;
    form with adverb ending in "-ly" is not hyphenated;

    ten-minute breaks, five-story buildings,
    densely populated, closely related;
