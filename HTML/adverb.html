Introduction

Adverb is a word that describes a verb, adjective, (another) adverb or clause.

Man walked out silently. (verb)
Emily is very beautiful. (adjective)
Train moved too slowly. (adverb)
Finally, we arrived. (clause)

Adverbs answers following questions:
how, when, where, why, how long, how often.

Adverb Categories

There are many adverbs. We divide them into categories for convenience.
There are adverbs of:

  • Manner

    They tell you how.

    very, also, slowly;
    The car stopped suddenly.
    My coffee is too hot.

  • Time

    They tell you when.

    lately,   early,   soon;
    We are ready now.
    See you later.

  • Place

    They tell you where.

    here,   away,   downstairs;
    Children are playing outside.
    My brother lives abroad.

  • Reason

    They tell you why.

    because,   since,   so;
    We returned, as it was getting dark.
    It's my birthday, hence the outfit.

  • Frequency

    They tell you how often.

    usually,   never,   seldom;
    I always wake up at 8 a.m.
    We meet each other regularly.

  • Duration

    They tell you how long.

    since,   for,   till;
    They haven't come yet.
    It took forever to finish this.

Additionally, there are flat adverbs, that look like adjectives.1

fast,   straight,   hard;
Turists arrived early. (adverb)
It was an early autumn. (adjective)

1 Adjective describes only a noun or pronoun.

Clause Adverbs

These adverbs primarily describe a clause:
thus, however, nonetheless, nevertheless, furthermore, therefore, moreover, meanwhile, etc,.
Clause adverb usually:

  • starts the clause it modifies;
  • has a comma after itself;

Initially, project was approved .
So, it received funding.
However, we found flaws in it.

When located between two clauses, it is called a conjunctive adverb, which:

  • is preceded by semicolon and followed by comma:

    Enemy attacked at night; however, we were ready.

  • is generally a bad idea, because we have conjunctions to join clauses:

    Enemy attacked at night, but we were ready.

  • we can use to split that compound sentence:

    Enemy attacked at night. However, we were ready.

Adverbial Phrase

It is a phrase in the sentence, that functions as an adverb.
That is, it answers one of adverbs questions.

Turists can get there by bus. (how)
She dined in a nearby cafe. (where)
The train arrived in the morning. (when)
People work to make a living. (why)
I meet Lucy now and then. (how often)
We've waited till midnight. (how long)

Adverb Suffixes

Below suffixes often indicate an adverb.
