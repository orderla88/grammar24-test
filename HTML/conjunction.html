Introduction

Conjunction is a word, that connects parts of sentence, such as:

  • words;

    We bought fruits and vegetables.

  • phrases;

    I’m older than Amy but younger than Jack.

  • clauses;

    You should behave, or I’ll call your parents.

There are 4 types of conjunctions:

  1. Coordinative;
  2. Correlative;
  3. Subordinative;
  4. Compound;

Coordinative Conjunction

It connects grammatically equal items,1 such as:

  • similar words;

    Do you prefer cats or dogs.  (nouns)
    I like singing and dancing.  (gerunds)

  • similar phrases;

    To be or not to be... .
    He has a kind heart and big muscles.

  • independent clauses;

    I called twice, but nobody answered.
    The air was chilly, yet we liked it.

7 Coordinative Conjunctions:
and Jack and Jill went up the hill.
but The day was warm but cloudy.
for Let him in, for he is my friend.
not We ordered tea, not coffee
or You can pay with cash or by card.
so It’s 9 p.m. so I’m a bit tired.
yet Ben is a rich yet thrifty man.
1 Connecting grammatically equal units is called a parallelism.

Correlative Conjunction

It comes as pair of conjunctions.
Connected items (words/phrases/clauses) come after each conjunction of the pair.

7 Correlative Conjunctions:
as...as Take as much money as you need.
both...and She is both smart and beautiful.
either...or Choose either me or your friends.
neither...nor Josh neither called nor visited us.
not only...but also Not only was it rainy, but also chilly.
rather...than I would rather die than marry her!
whether...or It’s whether yes or no, not maybe.

Subordinative Conjunction

It connects independent clause with dependent clause, which provides supplemental information to the former. Subordinative conjunction is a part of dependent clause2.
The sentence structure can be following:

  1. Independent + Conjunction + Dependent.

    Our team won the game as I expected.

  2. Conjunction + Dependent, + Independent.

    As I expected, our team won the game.

In the 1st case comma is optional, unless there is a contrast.
In the 2nd case comma is mandatory.

2 Dependent/independent clauses sometimes are called subordinate/main clauses accordingly.

Subordinative conjunctions are categorized by relations they connect clauses. Some conjunctions may belong to several categories.

Subordinative Conjunctions
Cause and Effect
as As you said, the beach was crowded.
because We were late because Bob's car broke.
since Since we're ready, let's get going.
so It was a good offer so I accepted it.
Time
after After party ended, he drove her home.
before The show was over before everyone knew.
once Never come back once you leave.
still We called him twice, still no answer.
till I will love you till the world ends.
until Let’s wait here until emergency arrives.
when Everyone laughed when they saw my outfit.
whenever Hannah is busy whenever I see her.
while Mom was cooking while we played outside.
Place
where Do you know where my keys are?
wherever Cops will find you wherever I are.
Condition
if I will be upstairs if someone asks.
only John is rich, only nobody knows it.
unless They won't know unless we tell them.
Contrast
although Although it is late, stores are open.
lest Hold onto rails lest you may fall.
than That's more money than we imagined.
though I saw him, though he didn’t notice me.
whereas I like coffee, whereas Mom prefers tea.

Compound Conjunction

It is a phrase of 2-3 words, acting as a single conjunction. The structure can be:

  • conjunction + conjunction;
  • conjunction + other PoS;

Like other types of conjunctions, compound conjunction connect words, phrases and clauses.

so that Buy some vegies, so that I make a salad.
now that Now that we arrived, you can rest.
as if He greeted me as if we knew each other.
as though Lucy looked as though she was ill.
in case Take an umbrella in case it's raining.
only if Ask for help only if you really need it.
even if I don't care even if he was a Pope.
even though Even though Joe was fired, he wasn't sad.
as long as We can play as long as we want.
as well as She owns a car as well as a motorbike.
as soon as Call me as soon as you arrive there.
provided that I'll come, provided that she wiil be there.

Conjunction vs Preposition

Conjunction and preposition have some overlapping words. Here's how to tell what the word is:

  • Preposition is followed by word or phrase (its object);

    We must return before sunset.
    People waited untill 2 a.m.

  • Conjunction is followed by a clause;

    We must return before sun goes down.
    People waited untill clocks passed midnight.

Similarities:

  • both connect words and phrases in the sentence;
  • both belong to minor Parts of Speech;

Differences:

  • Preposition cannot connect clauses.
  • Preposition can form an expression with other PoS. Whereas conjunction usually stands alone (except of compound one).
