Definition

Noun is a word, that represents:

  • concrete, physical object;

    laptop, Robert, sidewalk

  • abstract concept or idea;

    permission, knowledge, success

Making Plural
  • Singular noun refers to one object;
  • Plural noun refers to more than one object;

To turn singular noun into plural one, we generally add suffixes -s/-es to it. Specifically, if noun ends in:

  • ch, sh, s, ss, x ↠ add suffix -es;
    branch branches
    dish dishes
    bus buses
    glass glasses
    box boxes
  • consonant+y ↠ replace -y with -i, then add -es;
    vowel+y ↠ add only -s;
    baby babies
    cherry cherries
    cowboy cowboys
    day days
  • consonant+f ↠ replace -f with -v, then add -es;
    vowel+f ↠ add only -s;
    calf calves
    elf elves
    chef chefs
    roof roofs
  • consonant+o ↠ add -es;
    vowel+o ↠ add only -s;
    tomato tomatoes
    potato potatoes
    cameo cameos
    radio radios

In addition, there are nouns that:

  • have irregular plural forms;
    child children
    man men
    mouse mice
    person people
    tooth teeth
  • have same single and plural forms;
    bison bison
    deer deer
    fish fish
    sheep sheep
  • always written in plural form;1
    to make them single use "a pair of" phrase;
    single plural
    a pair of glasses glasses
    a pair of pants pants
    a pair of scissors scissors
    a pair of trousers trousers
1 These nouns are usually tools/clothes that have two working parts, like trousers with two legs.

Pronunciation

  • The suffix -s is pronounced as either [z] or [s], depending on previous voiced/voiceless sound.
  • The suffix -es is pronounced as [ɪz], unless the word ends in -e.
jeans [ˈdʒiːnz]
cats [kæts]
glasses ['glɑːsɪz]
apples ['æplz]

Noun Genders

Nouns and some pronouns 2 can be categorized into 4 grammatical genders:

  • Masculine gender for animate male objects;
  • Feminine gender for animate female objects;
  • Common gender for both male and female objects;
  • Neuter gender for inanimate objects or concepts;

Collective Nouns

Collective noun is a word for a group of objects.

bunch, crowd, pack, family;

  • It is often followed by a prepositional phrase "of ... ", which specifies the kind of object.
    array of (numbers, letters)
    dozen of (eggs, cookies)
    gang of (bikers, robbers,)
    heap of (sand, clothes)
    pack of (cigarettes, wolves)
    pair of (glasses, gloves)
    troupe of (actors, clowns)
  • In American English it is mostly treated as a single noun, taking single verb.

    A nest of birds lives on the tree.
    A troupe of actors lives in trailer.

  • In British English it can be treated as either single or plural noun, depending on the context.
    The team was happy to see me. (whole unit)
    The team were happy to see me. (each member)
  • Here are collective nouns for some animal groups.
    brood of (chicks, ducklings)
    colony of (penguins, ants)
    herd of (sheep, goats)
    litter of (kitten, cubs)
    nest of (snakes, rabbits)

Compound Nouns

A compound noun is a single noun, made by joining two or more words together. These words are mostly a mix of noun with other PoS. Types of compound nouns:

  • Closed – noun/adjective + noun written as a single word;

    homework, bedroom, toothpaste

  • Open – adjective + noun written separately;

    washing machine, living room,
    swimming pool, high school;

  • Hyphenated – various PoS joined by a hyphen. Only the noun part is pluralized if present;
    daughter-in-law, take-off, self-control;
    daughters-in-law, take-offs, self-controls;
