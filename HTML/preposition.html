Introduction

  • Preposition is a word, that connects following it word(s) to rest of the sentence.
    The following it word/phrase is called an object of preposition. It is usually a noun or noun phrase.
    Prepositional phrase is a preposition + its object. Prepositions connect words mostly in relations of time or place.

    Let's meet at 3 p.m.  (time)
    Let's meet at the cafe.  (place)

    • "to" is preposition;
    • "3 p.m." and "the library" are objects of prepositions;
    • "at 3 p.m." and "at the library" are prepositional phrases;

  • Main 3 prepositions

    They are at, in, on
    Each may connect in relation of time or place.

  • Preposition vs Adverb

    Many prepositions may also function as an adverb. Here's how to tell them apart:

    • preposition is always followed by its object;
    • adverb performs its function alone;
    They stayed inside the house.1 (preposition)
    They stayed inside. (adverb)
1 The prepositional phrase "inside the house" acts as an adverbial in sentence.

Categories

Prepositions connect words in relations of time, place, etc. We can categorize them by those relations. Some prepositions may belong to several categories.

    ago It happened a long time ago.1
    amid(st) He resigned amidst rumors of fraud.
    at Train arrives at 5 o’clock.
    before Work was done before evening.
    by All guests arrived by 9 p.m.
    circa This castle dates to circa 750 A.D.
    during Bees are busy during summer.
    for We rented bicycles for 2 hours.
    from Bank works from Monday to Friday.
    in The show starts in 10 minutes.
    on My dad is on vacation now.
    past It’s already half past midnight
    since It’s been raining since morning.

    within Ambulance arrived within an hour.

Expressions

Prepositional expression is a useful combination of preposition with other Part of Speech. Below are common expressions.

Verb + Preposition
Verb Prep-n Example
talk, hear about Have you heard about the news?
look, stare at The lady stared at me for awhile.
come, differ from Wolves greatly differ from dogs.
look, pay for He paid for me at the restaurant.
give, result in Eventually she gave in to his desires.
depend, hold on Kittens depend on mother-cat’s milk
belong, lead to This house belongs to my sister.
agree, deal with I totally agree with your opinion.
