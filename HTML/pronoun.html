Introduction

Pronoun is a word, that stands in for another word in the sentence. It can replace a noun, adjective, adverb or numeral. Replaced word is called pronoun's antecedent.

Pronoun Antecedent P.o.Sp
it dog noun
this nearby adjective
somehow easily adverb
which first numeral

In other words, pronoun unspecifically refers to an object, its quality/quantity or the way something is done.

Grammatical Person

This term helps to categorize personal pronouns. If I told you about someone/something, then grammatically speaking:

  1. I am (the talking one) called the 1st person.
  2. you are (the listening one) called the 2nd person.
  3. he/she/it is (we talk about) called the 3rd person.
Person Single Plural
1st I we
2nd you you
3rd he/she/it they

Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns consist of four groups:

  1. Subject pronouns can act as a subject in the sentence. Note the antecedents in the examples below.
    Person Singular Plural
    1st I we
    2nd you you
    3rd he/she/it they

    The tornado! It is moving towards us!
    Paul told me that he will be late.

  2. Object pronouns can act as an active object or complement in the sentence.
    Person Singular Plural
    1st me us
    2nd you you
    3rd him/her/it them
    He invited us there. (object)
    He told us the news. (compl-nt)
  3. Possessive pronouns represent possessive form of personal pronouns and consist of 2 subgroups:
    1. the 1st subgroup members can act as a determiner in the sentence;
      Person Singular Plural
      1st my our
      2nd your your
      3rd his/her/its their

      My brother loves music.
      His favorite band is Beatles.

    2. the 2nd subgroup members can act as a subject or passive object in the sentence;

      Person Singular Plural
      1st mine ours
      2nd yours yours
      3rd his/hers/its theirs
      This old truck is mine. (object)
      Mine is older than yours. (subject)
  4. Reflexive Pronouns end with suffix -self and refer back to the subject of sentence. They can act as either an object or adverbial.
    Singular
    1st myself
    2nd yourself
    3rd himself/herself/itself
    Plural
    1st ourselves
    2nd yourselves
    3rd themselves
    The cat licks itself after meal. (object)
    I made this cake by myself. (adverbial)
Summary
Person Subject Object Possessive Reflexive
Singular
1st I me my/mine myself
2nd you you your/yours yourself
3rd he/she/it him/her/it his/her/its him/her/it+self

1st we us our/ours ourselves 1
2nd you you your/yours yourselves

1 Irregular versions "ourself" and "themself" are used in specific cases.

Interrogative Pronouns

Interrogative pronouns consist of two groups:

  1. a group of question words: who, whom, whose, what, which, when, where, how;
  2. a group, formed by attaching suffix -ever to the question words;
Pronoun Example
who
whoever		 Who the hell are you?
I don't care whoever you are.
whom
whomever		 Whom did you see?
Give this to whomever you like
whose
whosever		 Whose team is winning?
Whosever idea it is, I like it.
what
whatever 2		 What is your name?
Take whatever you want.
which
whichever		 Which bags is yours?
Pick whichever color you like.
when
whenever		 When do we start?
She is busy whenever I see her.
where
wherever		 Where is Lauren?
Take a sit wherever you like.
how
however		 How do feel today?
Do it however you want.
2 An intensive form "whatsoever" can replace adverb "at all".

Indefinite Pronouns

Indefinite pronouns are formed:

  • by prepending prefixes no-, any-, some-, every-;
  • to roots one, body, thing, where, how, way;
any- every- no- some-
anybody everybody nobody somebody
anyone everyone none someone
anything everything nothing something
anywhere everywhere nowhere somewhere
anyhow everyhow 3 nohow 3 somehow
anyway everyway 4 noway 4 someway 4

Note that:

  • 3 "everyhow, nohow" are considered either nonstandard or archaic nowadays;

    Wind scattered the leaves everyhow.
    I can't figure this out nohow.

  • 4 "everyway, noway, someway" are often written separately nowadays;

    There is no way I can fix this.
    We tried every way to fix the machine.
    We need to find some way to fix it.

Demonstrative Pronouns

Pronouns this, these, that, those demonstrate/point at specific objects. In the sentence they can act as either a subject or determiner.

proximity single plural
nearby this these
farther that those

These are my keys, those are yours.
This car is faster than that one.

They are often called demonstrative adjectives. However, we know that adjectives do not have a number.
