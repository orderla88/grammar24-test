Introduction

Verb is a word for an action/state of someone or something.
Verb forms allow the verb to perform various (including non-tense) functions in the sentence. Below table sums up verb forms for a later reference.

Verb Forms

Verb forms

  • Base Form ↠ verb.
    A root form of verb without any modifications. In the sentence it can serve:

    • in predicate;

      I eat sandwich.

    • in imperative mood;

      Eat your breakfast!

    • with modal verbs;

      You must eat.

  • Infinitive ↠ to + verb.
    A base form, preceded by particle "to". In the sentence it can serve:

    • as subject;

      To live is the greatest adventure.

    • as object;

      He likes to run in the morning.

    • in determinal phrase;

      I had a chance to talk with him.

    • in adverbial phrase;

      Peter came to see Anna.

  • Present Participle ↠ verb+ing.
    Participates in forming progressive tenses. In the sentence it can also serve:

    • as subject;*

      Singing is my hobby.

    • as object;*

      I like singing.

    • as determiner;

      Cicada is a singing insect.

    • in adverbial phrase;

      He bathed, singing out loud.

    * Present participle used as a noun is called a gerund.

  • Past Participle

    Formula: verb+ed 2
    In the sentence it can also serve:

    • in perfect (completed) tenses;

      We has finished the work.
      Paul has drunk too much.

    • as passive object;

      The work was finished by 5pm.
      Paul was drunk today.

    • as a determiner;

      finished work,  drunk Paul;

  • Past Simple

    Formula: verb+ed 2
    Participates in forming Past Simple Tense.

    I opened the door.   (regular)
    I broke the window.   (irregular)

    2 Regular verbs only follow that formula. Irregular verbs form Past Participle and Past Tense forms without any specific formula.

Regular/Irregular

Past Participle and Past Simple forms divide verbs into regular/irregular ones.

  1. Regular Verbs

    • Follow the formula verb+ed to make Past Participle and Past Simple forms;

    • Make up the majority of English verbs;

    I opened the door. (Past Tense)
    The door is opened. (Past Participle)

    Note, that if verb:

    • ends in -e → we add only -d;

      create – created,   donate – donated

    • ends in consonant+y → we remove -y and add -ied;

      try – tried,   supply – supplied

    • has single syllable ending in vowel+consonant →
      we double the last consonant and add -ed;

      stop – stopped,   plan – planned

    2. Regular Verb Past Tense Past Participle
    close closed closed
    play played played
    drop dropped dropped

  2. Irregular Verbs

    • Do not follow any specific formula to make Past Participle and Past Simple forms;
    • Make up only 3% of English verbs. Although, the most used 10 verbs are all irregular;
    I broke my leg. (Past Tense)
    My leg is broken. (Past Participle)

Conjugation

Verbs are conjugated (change their form) to reflect a grammatical person.
All English verbs (except of "be") reflect only 3rd singlular person by getting either of endings -s/-es.

Person Single Plural
1st I verb. we verb.
2nd you verb. you verb.
3rd he/she/it verbs/es. they verb.

For example, let's conjugate "take":

Person Single Plural
1st I take we take
2nd you take you take
3rd he/she/it takes they take

Note, that if verb:

  • ends in -ss, -ch, -sh, -x, -z, -o, → we add -es;

    kiss - kisses
    brush - brushes

  • ends in consonant+y, → we replace -y with -ie and add -s;

    reply – replies
    deny – denies

  • ends in -o, → we add -es

    do - does
    go - goes

  • is have, → we remove -ve and add -s;

    He has a pen.
    Dog has a tail.

  • is modal or comes after such → we do not add anything;

    He can drive.
    She will sing.
