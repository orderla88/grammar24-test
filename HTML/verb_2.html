Auxiliary Verbs

Three auxiliary (a.k.a helping) verbs perform additional functions in English language.
They are – be, have, do.

  1. Be

    The most used English verb.

    • it is irregular;

      Verb Past Tense Past Participle
      be was/were been

    • conjugated in every person;

      Person Single Plural
      1st I am we are
      2nd you are you are
      3rd he/she/it is they are

    • participates in continuous tenses;

      The sun is setting down.
      It has been raining for hours.

    • participates in passive voice;

      The bridge is being repaired.
      It was damaged by hurricane.

  2. Have

    The 2nd most used English verb.

    • it is irregular;

      Verb Past Tense Past Participle
      have had had

    • conjugated in the 3rd single person by replacing ending -ve with -s;

      Person Single Plural
      1st I have we have
      2nd you have you have
      3rd he/she/it has they have

    • participates in perfect tenses;

      Dad has returned from work.
      I have been reading for an hour.

  3. Do

    The 3rd most used English verb.

    • it is irregular;

      Verb Past Tense Past Participle
      do did done

    • conjugated in the 3rd single person by adding -es;

      Person Single Plural
      1st I do we do
      2nd you do you do
      3rd he/she/it does they do

    • participates in interrogative sentences;

      Do you own this car.
      Does Kevin know about it.

    • participates in negative sentences;

      I do not own this car.
      He does not know about it.

Modal Verbs

A modal verb provides an additional meaning to the following verb.

I drive to school.
I can drive to school. (ability)
I must drive to school. (obligation)

Below are all modal verbs with the meaning they supplement.

Modal verb Meaning
can/could ability, possibility
may/might probability, permission
shall/should suggestion, necessity
will/would future/past, preference
must obligation, duty

The 2nd verb in pair is a Past Tense form.
Verb "must" does not have it.

can, could
I can ride a bicycle. ability
It could be here. possibility
Can I talk to you? permission
may, might
It might rain soon. probability
May I interrupt you? permission
shall, should
Shall we dance? suggestion
You should eat more. advice
We should go. necessity
will, would
Clients will arrive soon. future
Kids would often play here. past
I would rather stay home. preference
must
We must follow the rules. duty
Citizens must pay taxes. obligation

Phrasal Verbs

Phrasal verb is a verb + preposition/adverb.1 The preposition or adverb in this context is called a particle.
Phrasal verb has following characteristics:

  • It contains two or three words.

    give away,   turn on,
    get rid of,   run out of;

  • Its meaning usually differs from verb's own meaning.
    P.verb Meaning
    break up separate
    give in surrender
    look up search
    put off postpone
  • It is transitive/intransitive – it has/hasn't an object.

    I ran into my neighbor.  (transitive)
    Please, calm down.  (intransitive)

  • It is separable/inseparable – object can/cannot be put between verb and particle.

    Put your hands up!  (separable)
    Let's stick to the plan.  (inseparable)

1 Some include conjunction "with":
keep up with,   come up with;

Linking Verbs

Linking verb describes a state or condition.
It connects subject to describing/identifying it word(s). Think of it as an equal sign (=), which equates the subject to an idea.

The movie was interesting. (movie = interesting)
The dinner looks delicious. (dinner = delicious)

Linking verbs include:

  • Forms of "be"; am, is, are, have been, etc;

    My name is John.
    I am mechanic.

  • Sensory verbs: look, feel, smell, sound, taste;

    You look great today.
    The perfume smells good.

  • Stative verbs: become, appear, seem, remain, stay, turn;

    The future became unclear.
    Nevetheless I stayed calm.

Linking vs Action

For comparison, action verb describes an action.
It connects subject with word(s) receiving its action.

I prepared a dinner.
My mom tasted it.

Last example shows that some linking verbs may also act as action verbs.

Transitive/Intransitive

The ability to transmit an action onto an object separates verbs into 3 types.

  • Intransitive verb do not pass action onto object. Used without the object.

    Sam slept well tonight.
    Children laughed at joke.

  • Transitive verb pass action onto object. Used with the object.

    He threw the ball.
    She made a sandwich.

  • Ambitransitive verb can be used as both transitive/intransitive.

    The water boils at 100°C.   (intransitive)
    The cook boiled the water.   (transitive)

    He writes every day.   (intransitive)
    He writes a book.   (transitive)
