Introduction

Cambridge Dictionary gives following definition to a word:

“Word is a grammatical unit, that has a meaning, can be spoken and written."

Every word has a:

  • Spelling, which is determined by its graphemes;
  • Pronunciation, which is determined by its phonemes;
  • Meaning, which is determined by its morphemes;

Most of dictionaries provide them.

Spelling

Spelling is an ordered sequence of letters in the word.

rhythm = r-h-y-t-h-m = rythm
necessary = n-e-c-e-s-s-a-r-y

Letter is the smallest unit of written text.
Alphabet is a set of all letters used by particular language. English alphabet has 26 letters, which came from a Latin script. Standalone alphabet letter is considered a word, with its own pronunciation.

Grapheme is one or more letters, representing a sound. Different languages usually have different grapheme for particular sound (phoneme). English language uses following types of graphemes:

  • Graph – 1 letter like a, t, s;
  • Digraph – 2 letters like sh, ch, ee;
  • Trigraph – 3 letters like igh, tch,;
  • Quadgraph – 4 letters like ough;

Same sound may be represented by various graphemes.

Phoneme Grapheme Example

Pronunciation

Pronunciation is a way the language is spoken.
A branch of linguistics that studies human speech is called phonology. Branches of phonology:

  • Phonetics studies physical properties of human speech;
  • Phonemics studies pronunciation of specific language;
Phoneme is a sound particle of human language. Graphical representations of phonemes (sound symbols) are defined by IPA and are common for all languages. English language uses 44 phonemes:

  • 20 vowel;
  • 24 consonant;

Explanation

  • The mark ' in transcriptions is called a stress mark. It denotes stress on the following syllable.
  • Double vowel sounds aʊ, eɪ, eə, etc., are called diphthongs.
  • Vowels with a colon : are pronounced twice longer i.e. u: = uu,   a: = ɑɑ.
  • A little r at the end denotes an American accent, where we can:
    • add it instead of prolonging: ɑ:ʳ = ɑɑʳ = ɑr
    • replace the 2nd sound in diphthongs with it: eəʳ = er
  • In British English r-ending is pronounced only if the next word starts with vowel sound:

    pair of scissors   [peər əv 'sɪzəz]

Spelling vs Pronunciation

English is a non-phonetic language, which means that word's pronunciation and spelling do not always match. Although, in Middle Ages it used to be more phonetic like this:

folk – [folk],   knight – [kniht]

Over time pronunciation was changing while spelling was not.

Meaning

The meaning of a word is an idea, object, or feeling, that people agree to connect to it. Word's meaning consists of:

  • Denotation – an exact, literal dictionary definition of a word;
  • Connotation – a contextual tone of a word, based on the emotion attached to it;

Types of connotation:

Positive Neuter Negative
slim thin skinny
peculiar strange bizzare
vintage old outdated

Part of Speech (PoS) is a class of words, that share similar grammatical properties. The PoS of word is determined by its meaning and morphemes.
There are 10 PoS, divided into 2 types:

  1. Major PoS:
    • hold main meaning;
    • may change their form;
  2. Minor PoS:
    • hold supporting meaning;
    • do not change their form;
Major PoS
Noun apple, idea object or concept
Pronoun you, he replaces other words
Adjective blue, hungry describes noun
Verb do, take action or state
Adverb quickly, often describes action/state
Numeral five, zero numeric quantity
Minor PoS
Article an/the apple specifies noun
Preposition on, at connects words
Conjunction and, with connects clauses
Interjection oh, meh expresses reaction

In summary, the word consists of graphemes, phonemes and morphemes. In the next chapter we will learn about the morphemes.
